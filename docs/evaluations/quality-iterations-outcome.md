# Decision-quality iteration outcome, 2026-11-03

The experiments improved the measured operating point but **did not meet 95% precision, 90% recall and 85% negative coverage together**. Balanced-v1 and deployment remain unchanged. The final frozen policies below use the same 200-case development monitor: 59 native positives and 141 negatives. This validation has been inspected repeatedly; it is not fresh release evidence.

| -------------------------------- | ------: | --------------: | -----: | ----------------------: | --------------------: |

| Decider fusion, strict negative  |  43 / 0 |            100% |  72.9% |          70/141 = 49.6% |                     0 |
| Decider fusion, broader negative |  43 / 0 |            100% |  72.9% |         131/141 = 92.9% |                     5 |

Richer prompts helped some public cases. On the original SciFact dev pairs, Jev literal reaches 40 TP / 2 FP (95.2% precision, 56.3% recall), versus fresh balanced 33 / 2 (94.3%, 46.5%). VitaminC is mixed: literal reaches 33 / 4 (89.2%, 46.5%) versus balanced 29 / 3 (90.6%, 40.8%). All 399 fresh baseline payload hashes match the historical requests, while returned scores can vary. These are development observations with different formulations and negative policies, not a statistically established general improvement.

Increasing fit from 400 to 800 pairs preserves all original pairs and adds whole source components disjoint from validation and locked components. Decider refutation's training-selected cutoff moves from 0.85124 to 0.71737; frozen validation recall rises from 55.9% to 74.6%, with zero FP in both. Its default 0.8 point remains 35 TP (59.3%). More training data, fresh scores and changed canonical group/fold identities all contribute to this experiment, so training-size causality is not isolated. No validation threshold tuning occurs.

The remaining gap involves both ranking and composition. Final fusion AUROC is 0.9834 and Brier 0.0459, yet its frozen cutoff finds only 43/59 positives. A useful overall ranking does not guarantee the required tail precision and recall. Strict negative policies sacrifice coverage; broader policies falsely clear positives. Numeric vetoes can suppress strong semantic signals. Native rationale/revision labels also differ from literal-pair compatibility on inspected cases involving numeric bounds, property versus scope, association versus proven absence, or entity/time/comparator assumptions. Labels were not rewritten and no noise rate or impossible performance ceiling is inferred. More heads were not a reliable remedy: Jev five-head fusion became very conservative, and failed Boost variants recovered zero validation positives; their sources/results are preserved and the prototype was removed.

The next step is a service-contract labeling and model-adaptation loop. Assemble exact one-assertion × one-candidate pairs with documented human adjudication under the literal-compatibility rubric, preserving native labels separately. Prioritize quantitative bounds, aggregate/component scope, negation, statistical comparators, aliases and missing context. The [local audit](trusted-pair-label-audit.md) found no inspected existing human-confirmed true-pair corpus. Use training-only adjudicated hard cases for prompt/model-builder adaptation, freeze a small candidate family, and fit policies with canonical source-group separation and explicit false-clear budgets. Keep the reused validation as monitoring; evaluate untouched human-labeled cases only after the candidate and policy freeze. The human 500 / locked 200 gate remains unmet, and current locked labels remain unopened.
